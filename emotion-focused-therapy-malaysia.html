What is EFT?

Feelings carry
meaning.

Emotion Focused Therapy (EFT) attends to emotions, thoughts, bodily sensations, and the meanings we make of experience. Feelings can offer information about what matters to us, while some responses reflect earlier learning that is no longer helpful.

We explore a response in its context. Anger, for example, may signal a boundary violation or arise in response to another feeling. Its meaning is something we explore together, rather than assume.

Awareness

Notice.

Slow down and become aware of what you feel, including sensations, thoughts, and feelings that are hard to name.

Meaning

Understand.

Explore how your feelings connect with needs, beliefs, and past and present experiences.

Change

Work with emotion.

Work on regulation and new emotional experiences that may help shift painful, recurring responses.

We move between these aspects of the work according to your needs.

In a session

We work
together.

Sessions may include conversation, attention to bodily sensations, or experiential work such as a dialogue with a self-critical part of yourself. We discuss what fits your needs and readiness.

The therapist both follows your experience and offers guidance. We check what is helpful, support regulation when feelings become overwhelming, and adjust the pace together.

The aim includes emotional change as well as understanding. Outcomes and the time needed vary from person to person.

Questions about EFT

Before
we begin.

Is EFT only about talking about feelings?

EFT includes emotional experience, expression, reflection, regulation, and work towards changing unhelpful emotional responses. The focus depends on what is happening for you in the session.

What if I don't know what I feel?

You can begin with feeling confused, numb, overwhelmed, or unsure. Finding words for your experience can be part of the work.

Will every session use EFT?

EFT guides my work, but sessions are tailored to you. Other approaches may be integrated when clinically appropriate.

What does research say about EFT?

EFT has been studied in clinical trials, particularly for depression. Reviews suggest potential benefits, while also identifying limitations in study quality and representation. Evidence varies across concerns and does not establish that EFT is better than other therapies. Research informs our work alongside assessment, your preferences, and clinical judgment.

How do I know whether this is suitable for me?

An initial conversation helps clarify what you are looking for and whether this service may be a suitable next step.
