Emotion Focused Therapy in Malaysia

A way of working with emotion, meaning and what matters most.

Emotion Focused Therapy (EFT) is the main therapeutic orientation that guides my work at Side by Psych Wellness Centre. I am drawn to EFT because it is humanistic, emotionally attuned and interested in the deeper experiences underneath the patterns people struggle with.

Ask about therapyWhat is EFT?

Therapy is a shared process: thoughtful, paced carefully and shaped around your needs.

A grounded approach

EFT is not presented here as a one-size-fits-all solution. It is a thoughtful approach that helps inform the way therapy is understood and offered, while each person's care is tailored to their concerns, history and readiness.

What is Emotion Focused Therapy?

Emotions are not simply symptoms to get rid of.

In EFT, emotions are treated as meaningful signals. Even painful feelings can carry information about needs, wounds, values, fears and longings that have not yet found a safe way to be expressed.

Notice

Therapy may begin by slowing down and becoming more aware of what you are feeling in the moment, including the sensations and thoughts that come with it.

Understand

Together, we explore what an emotional reaction may be protecting, what it connects to, and why a familiar pattern may keep returning.

Respond differently

Over time, greater emotional clarity can support self-compassion, more direct communication, clearer boundaries and more flexible ways of relating.

Why this approach fits my work

Care that makes room for the inner world.

Many therapy approaches can be valuable. My work is primarily informed by EFT because its principles fit how I understand people: we are not broken, but can become stuck in emotional patterns that often developed for understandable reasons.

EFT allows space to look beyond the surface of a reaction. For example, anger may protect hurt; self-criticism may sit over shame or fear; numbness may have developed as a way to cope with too much. This is explored gently, without assuming that every experience has one simple explanation.

I have completed Level 3 training / certification in Emotion Focused Therapy. This training is an important part of my clinical development and informs my practice. It does not mean EFT is the only approach used in every session, nor does it replace the need to tailor therapy responsibly to each client.

When appropriate, I may integrate ideas from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, mindfulness and creative expressive approaches. The direction of therapy is based on the person in front of me, rather than a fixed protocol.

Who may find EFT-informed therapy helpful?

  • People who feel overwhelmed by emotions, anxious, low, shut down or disconnected from themselves.
  • People navigating self-criticism, shame, grief, loneliness, relationship difficulties or repeating interpersonal patterns.
  • People who want to understand the deeper meaning of their reactions, rather than only manage them at the surface.
  • People seeking a therapeutic relationship that is collaborative, compassionate and emotionally attentive.

In a session

Working carefully with what feels difficult.

An EFT-informed session may involve conversation, reflection, attention to bodily experience, gently exploring inner conflict, or finding words for feelings that have been hard to name. There is no expectation to perform emotion or to force a breakthrough.

Safety and pacing

The pace is adjusted carefully. It is important that difficult material is approached in a way that feels manageable, rather than overwhelming.

Collaboration

You remain an active participant. We check what is helpful, what feels unclear and what may need more time.

Meaningful change

Therapy may support new understanding and different responses, but outcomes vary and no particular result can be guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about EFT.

Is Emotion Focused Therapy only about talking about feelings?

No. EFT is not about simply expressing emotion. It involves understanding emotional patterns, their meaning and the needs or experiences beneath them, then working with them carefully in a therapeutic relationship.

Do I need to know exactly what I feel before starting therapy?

No. Many people come to therapy because their inner experience feels confusing, numb, intense or difficult to explain. We can begin from there.

Will EFT be used in every session?

EFT is the main orientation that guides my work, but therapy is tailored to each person. Other approaches, including ACT, mindfulness and creative expressive work, may be integrated where they are clinically appropriate.

Is EFT suitable for everyone?

No approach is a perfect fit for every person or concern. An initial conversation can help clarify what support you are looking for and whether this service may be suitable.
